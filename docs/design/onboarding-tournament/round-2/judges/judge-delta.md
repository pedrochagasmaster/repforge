## 3. Biggest strength and weakness

- **D.** Strength: it is the only candidate that is correct almost everywhere I pushed. Program-first reviews on every route, the accessible dialog and conflict handling, one accent, and zero feasibility cost. Weakness: the spec's own defaults combine badly. Corrections happen at the bottom of the page ("Montado com") and the change statement appears at the top, so a sighted lifter does not see what their correction did (`$S/cmp-corr390-b.png`). At 200% text the sheets are too small to work in (`$S/cmp-avrev320.png`).
- **E.** Strength: inline editing and route switching that can be undone. It is the most robust correction and avoidance interaction at 320/200, and a review that works as a hub. Weakness: the first viewport of the review is spent on the answer list, reproducing the L-3 problem the spec rejected. It needs PD-1 while being the slowest to Today (16 taps).
- **F.** Strength: the fastest honest path (13 taps) with no policy change, and the most truthful change statement (before/after reveals set changes). Weakness: the shared dialogs lose keyboard focus (a WCAG 2.4.3 failure on the cancel dialog, which is reachable from every view), and `custom-result` shows no program in its first viewport.

## 6. Implementation conflicts and hidden product decisions

- **E, PD-1 (declared, complete).** The header (`e.js:13-29`), policy (`e.js:1006`) and manifest agree. It relaxes K-19 only, and K-19 passed anyway. The declaration is accurate about what changes against Plan 054 AD.
- **E, `ROUTE_STEPS` order (partly declared as a "deviation", not as policy).** Recommend's `priorities` step becomes a review sub-state (`entry().step === "result"` while priorities are edited). A draft saved at `priorities` resumes on the review (notes, "O-10"). Production `program-entry.js` resume and validation would need to accept a result that exists before the priorities step. That is an architectural change the manifest does not list.
- **E and F, resume on the landing (undeclared).** The brand guide ("Generic … renders only while no program, content, or history exists and `entryLandingSeen` is not true … A later empty visit boots the ordinary Today/Program no-program states") and Plan 054 AD ("The generic landing appears once") mean a returning lifter with a draft never sees the landing. E's only resume surface is therefore unreachable in production unless the landing-once rule changes. F also shows the card on the chooser, so its dependency is partial. Spec O-10 lists "landing" as open but does not waive the landing-once rule. This is a hidden product-policy dependency for E and a softer one for F.
- **F, "Agora não" (O-7, allowed).** The implementation must restore the pre-link language (ADR 0007). It must also decide whether the `#setup=` fragment is cleared, since otherwise a reload shows the gate again. Neither is declared.
- **D.** No hidden decisions. Its "Continue enabled with an adjacent error on tap" follows the production validation pattern and is declared (notes, deviation 2). K-18 accepts it.

**Plain answer.** The best two are D and F. The choice between them is **not** mainly taste. D wins on correctness of recovery, accessibility and feasibility. F wins on activation cost and on the truthfulness and visibility of the change statement. Each loses on defects that are fixable and local, not structural. The evidence therefore supports a **synthesis**, not a pick. Once that synthesis is built, what remains is taste (landing composition, chips vs rows, the "Agora não" decline) plus one real policy question, PD-1. The evidence says the owner does not need to grant PD-1, because F's no-policy chooser already captures E's advantage.

## 9. Final recommendation

**Explicit synthesis on D's base.** No candidate is ready as-is.

1. **Base: D.** Keep its IA, `ROUTE_STEPS`, program-first review on every route, dialog and conflict focus handling, import-mode switch semantics, one-accent restraint, and resume on the chooser.
2. **From F: the chooser's featured block as Recommend's first question** (O-4, no product decision). This makes Rafael's path 13 taps. Keep D's helper link at the top of the chooser, not F's position at the bottom. Keep D's "Você faz / Você recebe" for the other doors.
3. **From F: the before/after count** (O-5), placed where the lifter is looking after an apply. Either scroll the review to the facts line and move focus to the statement after an apply, or place the statement next to the corrected fact. This fixes D-2.
4. **From E: inline expansion for answer editing** (O-9), at least at 200% text and 320 px. The evidence shows sheets fail there in both D and F (§5 D-A1, F-A2, D/F-A3).
5. **Fix D-1.** Show "Pular esta seção" only while the section is empty, or state what it removes.
6. **Shared fixes for every arm:** restore focus to the chosen option after a selection re-render; add a current-state reason (P048 #13); make the rules-drift button match its body copy.
7. **Leave out:** E's answers-first review (L-3), E's `aria-pressed` switches, F's and E's accent on Back and Cancel, and resume on the landing alone.

- **Without PD-1:** the synthesis above.
- **With PD-1 approved:** the recommendation is still the synthesis. Route-choice cost is PD-1's only benefit, and F's first-question chooser already delivers it (13 taps vs E's 16) without hiding the other four jobs behind a question screen. If the owner nonetheless wants to test PD-1, use E's inline editing and route switching but D's program-first review, and move resume off the landing.
- **Question for the owner:** "The first-question chooser already gives the saving PD-1 was meant to buy, without the policy change. Do you still want to test PD-1, or close it?" The design evidence alone resolves nothing else.
